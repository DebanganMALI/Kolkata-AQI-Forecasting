The year ahead, from the patterns of the last eight

Kolkata's air follows a strong yearly rhythm: smoggy from November to February, clean through the monsoon. The GRU learned that rhythm from 2018 to 2026. Starting from the latest official value (CPCB's daily bulletin), it forecasts one day, feeds that forecast back in, and keeps going, using Open-Meteo's weather forecast for the next two weeks and the average weather of each calendar day after that. This is an outlook, not live data, and the shaded range shows how far off this method was when we tested it on 2025 to 2026.

What is this project?

Every winter Kolkata's air turns "Poor" or worse for weeks at a time. A forecast one day ahead gives people time to act: keep children indoors, skip a morning run, plan around the worst days.

We forecast tomorrow's official AQI (India's National AQI from CPCB, the 24-hour value published at 4 PM) from the previous 14 days of air-quality and weather data, and raise a high-pollution alert when the forecast is above 200 ("Poor" or worse).

We compared eight forecasters, from linear regression to Transformers, against the simplest forecast, persistence ("tomorrow will be like today"). A model only counts as better when a statistical test says so. The chosen model, a GRU neural network, now runs this website every day.

What kind of learning is this?

  • Supervised learning. Every training example is a 14-day window of inputs labelled with the real AQI of the next day.
  • Regression, on a time series. The output is a number (the AQI), and the inputs are ordered in time.
  • Quantile regression. A second network predicts the 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles, which gives the 80% range.
  • Binary classification. A GRU classifier gives the probability that tomorrow's AQI is above 200.
  • Offline (batch) training. Models are trained on past data, then used. They do not keep learning while the site runs.
  • Deep learning. Recurrent networks (GRU, LSTM), attention, temporal convolutions and Transformers, trained by backpropagation through time.

It is not unsupervised learning (there are labels) and not reinforcement learning (no agent or reward).

Cleaning rules

  • The target is never filled in. The 4 days without an official AQI are never used as forecast targets.
  • Pollutant inputs are the daily mean over the stations reporting that day.
  • Input gaps of at most 7 days are carried forward and flagged. Longer gaps are not invented.
  • Boundary layer height was dropped: it is missing for January to June 2024, the validation year.
  • Low-confidence fires are dropped. We use the log count of fires within 300 km and the log fire power in the region.
  • Raw files are never modified. The tests check their bytes.

Neighbouring days are very similar. A random split would put days from the "future" into training and make every model look better than it is. The scaler (mean and spread of each input) is also learned from the training years only.

Step 1

Collect

Download CPCB city AQI and the 7 station files, Open-Meteo weather, NASA fire detections and the festival calendar.

Step 2

One daily table

Join everything by date into 2,953 rows, apply the cleaning rules and log every decision.

Step 3

14-day windows

Each sample is the previous 14 days of inputs, labelled with the next day's AQI. Windows only look backwards.

Step 4

Split and scale

Train, validation and test are blocks of dates. Scaling uses training statistics only.

Step 5

Train 24 models

8 forecasters x 3 input sets (pollution; plus weather; plus fires and festivals). Neural networks use 3 seeds each.

Step 6

Choose on 2024

The lowest mean validation error wins. All choices are saved to disk before the test years are touched.

Step 7

Test once

Score 2025 to 2026 once, against persistence on the same days, with a 95% block-bootstrap interval.

Step 8

Walk forward

Repeat the whole procedure with 2022, 2023 and 2024 as test years, each trained only on earlier data.

Step 9

Go live

Every evening, a scheduled job reads CPCB's new bulletin and Open-Meteo's weather, the GRU rolls the outlook forward, and the site republishes.

Why the GRU was chosen

  • By rule, not by hindsight. It had the lowest validation error on 2024 (MAE 12.9) of all 24 combinations, decided before the test years were seen.
  • Right size for the data. Two gates instead of the LSTM's three means about 25% fewer weights, so less overfitting on 1,918 training days.
  • Small and fast. 32 hidden units in one layer: it trains in seconds on the GPU and is light enough to run in plain NumPy for the live site.
  • Best alert. On the test years it had the best high-pollution alert score (F1 0.723 vs 0.585 for persistence) and R² 0.919.

The LSTM (pollution only) scored lower on test (11.2), but picking it after seeing the test results would be cheating, so we report it without choosing it.

The forecasting problem

ŷt+1 = fθ(xt−13, xt−12, …, xt)

xt is the vector of inputs on day t, and θ the weights learned in training.

Persistence:  ŷt+1 = yt
Change mode:  ŷt+1 = yt + Δ̂,   where the model predicts Δ = yt+1 − yt
Scaling:  x′ = (x − μtrain) / σtrain

GRU cell (as in PyTorch)

rt = σ(Wrxt + Urht−1 + br)   reset gate
zt = σ(Wzxt + Uzht−1 + bz)   update gate
nt = tanh(Wnxt + rt ⊙ (Unht−1 + bhn) + bn)
ht = (1 − zt) ⊙ nt + zt ⊙ ht−1
ŷ = wᵀ h14 + b

σ is the sigmoid and ⊙ element-wise multiplication. The term zt ⊙ ht−1 is an additive path that lets memory and gradients pass through many days, which eases the vanishing gradient problem.

LSTM cell, for comparison

ft, it, ot = σ(W xt + U ht−1 + b)   forget, input, output gates
ct = ft ⊙ ct−1 + it ⊙ tanh(Wcxt + Ucht−1 + bc)
ht = ot ⊙ tanh(ct)

Four weight sets against the GRU's three: about 25% more parameters for the same hidden size.

Training: loss and optimiser

L(θ) = (1/N) Σi (yi − ŷi)² + λ‖θ‖²
mt = β1mt−1 + (1 − β1) gt,   vt = β2vt−1 + (1 − β2) gt²
θ ← θ − η · m̂t / (√v̂t + ε)   Adam, η = 0.001

gt is the gradient from backpropagation through time; m̂ and v̂ are the bias-corrected averages. Gradients are clipped to norm 1.0.

Forecast range: pinball loss

Lτ(y, q) = max( τ(y − q), (τ − 1)(y − q) ),   τ ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 0.9}

Under-forecasting costs τ per point and over-forecasting costs 1 − τ, so the minimiser is the τ-th percentile. The 10th and 90th percentiles give the 80% range. The network is built so the three can never cross.

How we score

MAE = (1/N) Σ |yi − ŷi|    RMSE = √( (1/N) Σ (yi − ŷi)² )
R² = 1 − Σ (yi − ŷi)² / Σ (yi − ȳ)²
Precision = TP / (TP + FP),   Recall = TP / (TP + FN),   F1 = 2PR / (P + R)
Ensemble:  ŷ = ¼ (ŷGRU + ŷLSTM + ŷTransformer + ŷXGBoost)

Is the difference real? Block bootstrap

Δ = MAEmodel − MAEpersistence

The test days are resampled in blocks of 7 consecutive days, 2,000 times, and Δ is recomputed each time. The 2.5th to 97.5th percentiles give the 95% interval. Blocks keep the day-to-day correlation that a plain bootstrap would break. A model counts as better only if the whole interval is below zero.

How CPCB computes the AQI

Ip = (Ihi − Ilo) / (Bhi − Blo) · (Cp − Blo) + Ilo
AQI = maxp Ip

Cp is the 24-hour concentration of pollutant p, and Blo, Bhi are the breakpoints of its band. At least 3 pollutants are needed, one of them PM2.5 or PM10. The live "Government AQI now" averages this over Kolkata's stations.

Why a GPU?

The main experiment trains 24 model combinations, most with 3 seeds, plus learning curves, multi-day horizons and four walk-forward rounds: hundreds of networks. The GPU runs the matrix operations of each training batch in parallel, which made the whole study take minutes instead of hours. For a single forecast the GRU is tiny, so the live site needs no GPU.

Why not split the data at random?

Neighbouring days are very similar, so a random split lets the model see days around each test day during training. That leaks the future and inflates the score. We split by date: train up to 2023, validate on 2024, test on 2025 to 2026.

Why compare against persistence?

Air quality changes slowly, so "tomorrow equals today" is already a strong forecast. A model is only useful if it beats this free forecast, and we only claim it does when the 95% interval of the difference is entirely below zero.

Why a GRU and not an LSTM?

We did not pick by taste. Every model was scored on the 2024 validation year and the GRU had the lowest error. It also has two gates instead of three, about 25% fewer weights, which helps with only about 2,000 training days.

How did you prevent overfitting?

A small model (32 units, 1 layer), dropout 0.2, weight decay, early stopping on validation loss (patience 20), gradient clipping, choosing settings on validation only, and averaging 3 seeds.

What does MAE 11.8 mean?

On an average test day the forecast was 11.8 AQI points away from the official value. Persistence was 13.8 points away, so the GRU's error is about 14% lower.

What is the vanishing gradient problem, and how does the GRU help?

In a plain RNN the gradient is multiplied by the same weights at every time step, so it shrinks towards zero and the network forgets early days. The GRU's update gate gives an additive path, ht = (1 − z)·n + z·ht−1, so information and gradients can flow across many days.

What is backpropagation through time?

The network is unrolled over the 14 days like a 14-layer network with shared weights. The loss gradient is passed back through every day, and the gradients for the shared weights are added up.

Why 14 days of history?

It was fixed before any experiment. Two weeks covers two weekly cycles and the multi-day weather spells (calm, dry, cold air) that trap pollution in Kolkata's winter.

Did weather, fires and festivals help?

A little for the GRU (12.2 to 11.8), but for most models the difference was not statistically clear. Yesterday's AQI already carries much of the weather's effect.

Why use several seeds?

A neural network's result depends on its random starting weights. Training with seeds 42, 43 and 44 and averaging makes the forecast stable, and shows that a result is not luck.

Why is the error in November 2026 (23.4) higher than on the test years (11.8)?

November is when pollution rises fastest, so every method does worse: persistence was 28.1 over the same days. The live model also uses fewer inputs, because station pollutant files are not available every evening.

Why does this site show a different AQI from weather apps?

We use India's National AQI from CPCB: a 24-hour average with Indian breakpoints. Many apps show the US AQI or an hourly reading, which uses a different formula and time window, so they give different numbers for the same air.

What are the limitations?

The test years have cleaner air than most training years. Settings were chosen once, on 2024, and reused in every walk-forward round. Weather and fires show associations, not causes. Only two stations reported in 2018.

How it works. Data: official CPCB city AQI for Kolkata (2018 to 2026), 7 WBPCB monitoring stations, Open-Meteo weather and NASA FIRMS fire detections. Eight forecasters were compared; a GRU neural network was chosen on the 2024 validation year and tested once on 2025 to 2026. The ensemble averages the GRU, LSTM, Transformer and XGBoost forecasts.

Which AQI? All numbers are India's National AQI from CPCB: the 24-hour average published every day at 4 PM. Apps that show the US AQI or a live hourly reading use a different formula and time window, so they give different numbers for the same air.
